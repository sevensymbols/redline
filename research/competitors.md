## Gaps in the market the evidence shows
- Enterprise tools (Harvey, Luminance, Ironclad, Spellbook, Juro) are quote-only, six-figure or per-seat priced, and one source says Harvey is explicitly not for small firms. The individual/small-business end is served by the low-cost tools below, not these.
- Attorney-backed consumer services (Rocket Lawyer, LegalZoom) sit around $40/month but their top complaints are billing/cancellation, not review quality.
- The consumer/freelancer end already has multiple cheap or free AI readers that claim summaries, clause flags and suggested fixes. A plain summary plus risk flags is not differentiated on its own; I found no evidence on whether any offers per-clause counter-offers or document-grounded Q&A.
- Genie AI's free tier is criticised as a demo because exports are locked; a usable free tier is a visible pain point.
- Legal-claims risk is real: FTC action against DoNotPay was about unsubstantiated "AI lawyer" claims. Robin AI's collapse is a cautionary data point for "AI" products relying on hidden manual work.
- The ChatGPT/Claude substitute is the free alternative; cited objections are hallucination and confidentiality (from vendors with an interest).
